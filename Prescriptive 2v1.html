HTI's Covert Movement Still Exists, Although Banned Since 2017

Although it has been officially dissolved by the government on July 10, 2017 (through a Perppu), the activities of Hizbut Tahrir Indonesia (HTI) members allegedly continue to take place covertly. Former members and sympathizers of HTI remain active in spreading the ideology of the Islamic caliphate through various demonstrations and narratives that piggyback on actual issues, such as the fall of the Ottoman Turkish Caliphate and genocide in Palestine (using global issues to attract people's sympathy).
The concentration of this activity is observed quite strongly in a number of regions such as East Java, West Java, Central Java, South Kalimantan and Yogyakarta. Observers such as M. Najib Azca from PBNU and Muhammad Syauqillah from the University of Indonesia highlighted the pattern of the HTI movement that utilizes the sentiments of the people to attract support for ideologies that are contrary to Pancasila.
They use symbols such as the tawhid flag and infiltrate the issue of ummah as a strategy to continue to exist in the midst of an official ban. This situation demands vigilance and steps from the government so that the spread of this transnational ideology does not threaten the national consensus.

Notes

The government has also increased supervision of the activities of community organizations, taklim assemblies, and non-formal education institutions that are indicated to be channels for the spread of radical ideologies. On the other hand, ideological literacy and religious moderation programs continue to be promoted to strengthen people's understanding of the values of Pancasila as the basis of the state.
However, if the government fails to act consistently and firmly, then the space for groups like HTI will be increasingly open. The state's indecisiveness has the potential to expand the influence of the caliphate ideology in society, especially the younger generation who are vulnerable to extreme religious narratives. In the long run, this could threaten the stability of national ideology and lead to serious socio-political disintegration.

Densus 88 Arrests Suspected Terrorist in Purworejo, Allegedly Spreading Propaganda via Social Media

On Monday morning, May 26, 2025, the Indonesian National Police's Special Detachment 88 (Densus 88) Counterterrorism Unit arrested a 32-year-old man identified as Ahmad Fadhil at his home yard in Duluh Krajan, Kaliurip Village, Bener District, Purworejo Regency, Central Java. The arrest was part of counterterrorism efforts aimed at curbing the spread of radical ideologies growing on social media.
Ahmad Fadhil is strongly suspected of being a sympathizer of Jamaah Ansharut Daulah (JAD) Poso, a terrorist group affiliated with ISIS. Intelligence reports indicate Fadhil maintained direct contact with known terrorist figures including Mukhlis Hidayat, a JAD cell leader in Poso, and Ridwan Saputra, a bomb-making specialist previously linked to the 2018 Surabaya attacks.
During the search, the Densus 88 team found various suspicious evidence, including handwritten documents containing violent teachings, manuscripts on jihad jurisprudence (fiqh), laws on ghanimah (war booty), and other extremist doctrines. Additionally, officers confiscated violent-themed pamphlets, sharp weapons such as daggers and folding knives, as well as physical training equipment suspected to have been used in attack simulations.
This arrest is clear evidence that radical networks remain active in targeting youth and exploiting digital platforms as tools for recruitment and spreading violent ideology. Authorities reaffirm their commitment to continue dismantling terrorist sleeper cells and preventing the spread of radicalism within society.

Connected Terrorist Network

Key Figures in Fadhil's Network
Primary Contact: Mukhlis Hidayat
JAD Poso cell leader, age 45, previously detained in 2019 for recruitment activities. Released in 2023, suspected of rebuilding network operations through encrypted communications. Known for expertise in ideological indoctrination and tactical planning.
Explosives Specialist: Ridwan Saputra
Bomb-making specialist, age 38, linked to Surabaya church bombings in 2018. Currently at large, believed to be operating from East Java. Has been providing online training materials for homemade explosive devices through dark web channels.
Recruitment Coordinator: Farid Nurdin
Age 29, responsible for youth recruitment in Central Java region. Former university student radicalized in 2020, now operates multiple social media accounts using false identities. Specializes in targeting disaffected youth through gaming and social platforms.
Financial Coordinator: Ahmad Surya
Age 42, manages funding channels for terrorist activities through cryptocurrency and informal remittance systems. Previously worked in banking sector before radicalization in 2017. Maintains connections with foreign funding sources in Syria and Afghanistan.

Official Statements

Police Statement - Commissioner Ahmad Riyadhi
National Police Head of Public Information Division stated that this operation was the result of intensive surveillance for three months. "We have been monitoring Ahmad Fadhil's online activities and his connections with known terrorist networks including communications with Mukhlis Hidayat and Ridwan Saputra," Commissioner Ahmad Riyadhi announced during a press briefing.
Densus 88 Commander Statement
Densus 88 Commander, Brigadier General Farid Makruf, emphasized that the arrest demonstrates law enforcement's commitment to preventing domestic terrorism. "Ahmad Fadhil was actively recruiting through encrypted messaging platforms and had established a network of at least 15 sympathizers across Central Java, with direct guidance from Farid Nurdin," he revealed.

Network Operations Analysis

Terrorist Cell Structure and Operations
January 2025 - Initial Contact
Ahmad Fadhil first established contact with Mukhlis Hidayat through encrypted Telegram channels, beginning ideological indoctrination process and receiving instructions for local recruitment.
March 2025 - Technical Training
Ridwan Saputra provided Ahmad Fadhil with bomb-making manuals and instructional videos through secure file-sharing platforms. Evidence shows Fadhil attempted to procure chemical materials for explosive testing.
April 2025 - Recruitment Phase
Working with Farid Nurdin, Ahmad Fadhil successfully recruited 8 local youth through religious study groups that gradually introduced extremist ideology and physical training components.
May 2025 - Financial Support
Ahmad Surya transferred approximately 50 million rupiah to Ahmad Fadhil through cryptocurrency exchanges to fund weapons procurement and operational expenses for planned attacks.

Expert Analysis

Dr. Suharto Wijaya - Indonesian Institute for Strategic Studies
"The involvement of figures like Mukhlis Hidayat and Ridwan Saputra demonstrates the resilience of terrorist networks. Despite previous arrests, these groups continue to operate by adapting their communication methods and recruitment strategies," said Dr. Suharto Wijaya.
Professor Indira Sari - University of Indonesia Criminology
"The collaboration between Ahmad Fadhil, Farid Nurdin, and Ahmad Surya shows how modern terrorist cells combine traditional religious extremism with sophisticated financial and technological capabilities. This represents a significant evolution in domestic terrorism threats," she explained.

Government Response

Coordinating Minister for Political, Legal and Security Affairs
Minister Wiranto Pranowo announced increased coordination between Densus 88, BNPT, and regional governments. "The arrest of Ahmad Fadhil has provided valuable intelligence on the network involving Mukhlis Hidayat, Ridwan Saputra, Farid Nurdin, and Ahmad Surya. We are implementing a comprehensive approach to dismantle their entire operation," he stated.
BNPT Chief Statement
BNPT Chief, Lieutenant General Agus Setiawan, confirmed ongoing operations to apprehend the remaining network members. "We have identified the locations of Farid Nurdin and Ahmad Surya, while international cooperation is being pursued to track Ridwan Saputra's current whereabouts," he announced.
