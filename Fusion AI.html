HTI's Covert Movement Still Exists, Although Banned Since 2017

Facts

Although it has been officially dissolved by the government on July 10, 2017 (through a Perppu), the activities of Hizbut Tahrir Indonesia (HTI) members allegedly continue to take place covertly. Former members and sympathizers of HTI remain active in spreading the ideology of the Islamic caliphate through various demonstrations and narratives that piggyback on actual issues, such as the fall of the Ottoman Turkish Caliphate and genocide in Palestine (using global issues to attract people's sympathy).
The activities and spread of HTI's ideology can still be found in certain WhatsApp groups, mosques, and communities that have been infiltrated by its members. According to former HTI member Solihin Abdul Wahab, there are at least 300-500 former HTI members who remain active in West Java alone. Throughout Indonesia, the estimated number is in the thousands.
The Indonesian government continues to monitor the activities of former HTI members who are trying to revive the organization. Some former members have joined other Islamic organizations to continue their ideological struggle. At least 11 Islamic organizations are suspected of being infiltrated by former HTI members as part of their strategy to maintain the caliphate ideology.

Analysis

The persistence of HTI's activities post-dissolution demonstrates the organization's resilience and adaptability. Their strategy of infiltrating legitimate Islamic organizations and utilizing digital platforms indicates a sophisticated approach to circumventing government restrictions. The involvement of former high-ranking members like Ismail Yusanto (former HTI spokesperson) in continuing to spread the ideology through various channels suggests a well-coordinated underground network.
However, if the government fails to act consistently and firmly, then the space for groups like HTI will be increasingly open. The state's indecisiveness has the potential to expand the influence of the caliphate ideology in society, especially the younger generation who are vulnerable to extreme religious narratives. In the long run, this could threaten the stability of national ideology and lead to serious socio-political disintegration.

AF is strongly suspected of being a sympathizer of Jamaah Ansharut Daulah (JAD) Poso, a terrorist group affiliated with ISIS. Authorities reaffirm their commitment to continue dismantling terrorist sleeper cells and preventing the spread of radicalism within society.

Young Papuan figure accuses Sebby Sambon of having no education in international humanitarian law

Facts

Otis Tabuni, a young figure from Timika, Central Papua, has strongly criticized Sebby Sambon, spokesperson for the West Papua National Liberation Army - Free Papua Organization (TPNPB-OPM). In his statement on August 6, 2024, Otis considered that Sebby and his group did not understand or ignore the principles of international humanitarian law that should be the basis for armed conflict.
Otis highlighted a number of TPNPB-OPM actions that he considered to have exceeded the limits of a dignified struggle. He mentioned that attacks on civilians, schools, and health care facilities such as health centers are serious violations of the Geneva Convention. In the law of war, attacks can only be directed against combatants or military parties, not against non-combatants or civilians.
Otis' statement came as a form of concern over the escalating violence in Papua and the deteriorating image of the armed groups' struggle. He reminded that in any endeavor, including political or independence struggles, universal human values must be upheld. For Otis, losing the moral direction of the struggle will undermine the original goal and exacerbate the suffering of innocent civilians.

Notes

The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) should immediately oversee the application of international humanitarian law, provide training and education to parties to the conflict, and conduct humanitarian missions to protect and assist victims of the conflict. Certain groups, such as TPNPB-OPM, could face a strong response from the international community and the Indonesian government if they continue to commit acts that violate humanitarian law, including the possibility of being categorized as terrorist groups or war criminals.
Similar patterns have occurred elsewhere, such as the conflict in Sri Lanka with the Tamil Tigers, where violations of humanitarian law by armed groups led to massive military operations and significant humanitarian impacts. If violations of international humanitarian law by armed groups are not prevented, the potential impact is increased civilian casualties, human rights violations, and possible international intervention due to conflict escalation.

Notes
